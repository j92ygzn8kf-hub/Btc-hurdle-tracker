How this works

For your chosen horizon of N years, we take every rolling N-year window in the data (for example 2012–2017, 2013–2018, and so on), compute the compound annual growth rate of Bitcoin in each window, and count how often it beat your expected return. That count is the hurdle: the percentage of historical periods your alternative would have had to beat.

BTC prices are approximate year-end closes, snapshot through September 2026. The final data point is September 2026, not a full year-end.
